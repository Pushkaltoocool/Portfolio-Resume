# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters, internship/job interviewers, competition judges, and potential collaborators evaluating Pushkal Vashist — an AI/cloud engineering student in Singapore (NYP, Cloud Computing president, DIS Sentinel apprentice). They arrive from LinkedIn or a resume link, usually on a laptop, and skim: who is this, what has he shipped, has he won anything, how do I reach him.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio that proves engineering competence by being an engineered artifact itself: 3D hero, live projects, competition wins. The blog extends this into a public dev log (what he's building and learning); the booking page turns interest into a scheduled conversation without email back-and-forth. Success = a visitor books a call or reaches out.

## Brand Personality

Technical, ambitious, kinetic. A dark "mission-control" space: near-black surfaces, precise white typography, restrained blue/purple energy, terminal motifs. Confident but student-genuine — trophies and shipped demos, not corporate gloss.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing-page grammar: identical card grids, gradient-text heroes, eyebrow kickers over every section.
- Notion-style pastel minimalism — this brand is dark and kinetic.
- Template portfolio clones with stock "designer" serifs; the voice is engineered, not editorial.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach** — every new surface (blog, booking) should itself demonstrate engineering craft.
2. **Dark is the identity** — near-black (#010103) base, light type, color used as energy accents, never as decoration flood.
3. **Terminal honesty** — monospace for metadata/code, real data (dates, timezones, busy blocks) shown plainly.
4. **One idea per fold** — long scroll, deliberate pacing, no cramming.
5. **Fast and functional beats flashy** — 3D and motion never block reading, booking, or publishing.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target, but: body text ≥4.5:1 on dark surfaces, keyboard-reachable booking flow and editor, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected for entrance animations, form errors announced in text (not color alone). All times shown with explicit timezone labels.
